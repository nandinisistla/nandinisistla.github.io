11 · Fairness

No app, no wifi, no power, and no reading required.

Five languages chosen from who actually lives in Alphabet City.

Five languages, chosen from the censusEnglish, Spanish, Chinese, French and Ukrainian, reflecting the Puerto Rican, Slavic, Chinese and Francophone communities most exposed to flood risk.
An open resourceDesigned for all residential buildings, regardless of income, tenure or immigration status.
Icons carry the sequenceNo reading required to follow the core protocol, so it works for children, seniors and non-English speakers.
Physical by defaultNo app, no wifi, no power. Nobody is excluded by the device they do not own.

12 · The pitch

Life-saving knowledge, embedded in daily life.

Most flood emergency solutions in New York assume residents have English fluency, tech literacy and working infrastructure. In neighbourhoods like Alphabet City, none of those are guaranteed.

The Heimlich poster has saved lives for decades because people absorb it over years of exposure. Simple, reinforced visuals empower action in an emergency.

Rivas Flood Protocol is designed for apartments in high flood risk zones. It works for children, seniors and those whose first language is not English. Residents get life-saving knowledge embedded in daily life.
