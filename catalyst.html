Catalyst: designing a two sided investing platform

RoleProduct Design (UI/UX) Intern
TimelineJul 2026 to present
ToolFigma
Scope100+ mobile screens, design system
01

The product

Catalyst gives everyday consumers access to pre-IPO and private startup investment opportunities, and connects founders with mission-aligned capital. Two audiences with opposite goals, moving through one product.

I joined as the Product Design (UI/UX) Intern, working closely with the CEO, and designed the interface for the web and mobile platform. Catalyst launches August 2026.

02

One product, two sides

Every surface had to resolve twice. A founder and an investor arrive with different vocabulary and different definitions of a good match, but they share a codebase and a brand.

The system draws the line in a consistent place. Structure is shared, content forks.

Five step onboarding spine, one progress indicatorCompany basics, stage, industry, legal agreementInvestor type, accreditation, sectors, cheque size, thesis
One saved card anatomySaved investors, cheque size leadsSaved startups, valuation leads

03

Three directions

I explored three distinct design directions and presented them to the founding team. We selected one, and I took it into production and built the system out from there.

Choosing before building meant the components settled early, and the remaining screens could be assembled rather than redrawn.

04

The design system

I defined typography, colour, glassmorphism surfaces, avatar style, buttons, spacing, and navigation.

TypeFraunces carries display and section headings. Inter carries body copy and interface labels. Two faces, four weights, one scale.
ColourA near black base (#0A0A0D) and bone white (#F6F5F2) for primary actions. Gold (#C6A02C) does one job only: status. Verified, raising, leads rounds, Pro. Because it never decorates, it always means something.
SurfacesOne glass card does all the work: 20 px blur, 1 px hairline border, inset top highlight, 18 to 20 px radius. Traction, funding, team, deal flow and settings groups are all the same card holding different content.
ChipsA 20 tag industry taxonomy and a four option stage selector, defined once and reused in onboarding, on profiles, and in discovery filters.
05

Onboarding

Five steps, forking by user type. The founder path runs company basics, then stage and industry, then the legal agreement.

Stage and industry are tappable chips rather than dropdowns, so the full set of options is visible at once and selection is a single thumb tap. The screen also states why it is asking: “Investors filter by these.” The same taxonomy powers the filtering later, so the answer is not busywork.

Progress stays visible across all five steps, required fields are marked, and continue stays disabled until they are filled, so the user is never guessing how far along they are or why they are stuck.

06

The founder profile: equal stats, never empty

This was the hardest module. A funded, post revenue founder has MRR, growth, paying customers and burn to show. A pre revenue founder has none of it, and built the obvious way their traction card renders empty or half filled directly beside a funded profile.

“Traction: equal stats, never empty.”  /  “Even row of equal stats, no hero number.”  /  “Pre-revenue stats: same shape, different proof.”design notes, founder profile component
ModulePost revenuePre revenue
Traction, four equal stat cardsMRR, growth, customers, burnWaitlist signups, live pilots, signed LOIs, burn
FundingRound, amount, lead, additional backersAmount sought and runway remaining

Four equal stat cards either way, no size hierarchy, no hero number. Not raised shows a position, not a blank.

07

Saved lists

Saved lists mirror across the marketplace: founders save investors, investors save startups. One card anatomy, two payloads.

Each card leads with the figure that side actually scans for. Founders browsing investors see cheque size. Investors browsing startups see valuation. Stage, sector count, whether an investor leads rounds, growth rate, location and lead investor sit below as supporting detail, in a fixed order so the eye learns where to look.

08

Tracking tools, and their limits

Both sides get a tracking surface, and both carry a warning that names precisely what the tool is not.

The investor portfolio is self reported and not legally binding. The founder cap table is manual only, and SAFE agreements must be executed off platform. In both cases the caveat sits above the first number rather than under the last one.

09

Messages

Conversation is where the marketplace either works or stalls, so the inbox is built for triage. Filters and a requests tab come before the list, and the company summary stays pinned above the thread.

10

Verification and disclosure

Strangers evaluate each other here before money moves, so verification is part of the interface rather than a footnote.

Settings carry government ID verification, reviewed manually and required to unlock full platform access. Founders disclose legal company name, EIN, state of incorporation and company address. Pitch deck visibility is an explicit choice, not a default: public on Discover, or private and shared on request.

Catalyst does not handle or facilitate the exchange of funds or securities. All transactions happen off platform through legal and financial channels. That appears in the onboarding agreement and again in settings, stated plainly in both places.

11

Scope

100+ mobile screens in Figma: login, onboarding, home, dashboards, cap tables, founder and investor profile cards, checklists, saved lists, verification and settings, all assembled from the one system, and specified for the developers building the app.
