Coat for a Teammate: Designing for a Real Person

01 · The Problem

Build an ideal coat for my teammate based entirely on understanding her specific needs, wants, and aspirations rather than assumptions.

Her needs: Extreme warmth for 15-minute walks. Deep center pockets for hands, phone, keys, and wallet. A hood that does not catch on her backpack. A high collar above the collarbone so she does not need a scarf. One coat with no layering. A quality zipper.

Her wants: A red coat she does not yet own. Matte finish. Monochrome only. Quirky details: asymmetrical zipper, thumb loops. Clean design with no ribbons, thin puff lines, or fussy elements. Detachable hood.

Her aspirations: Get compliments the way her unique blue Aritzia coat does. Express freedom after 13 years in school uniform. Stand out with less typical colors and details. Feel ready and confident for busy student days. Navigate NYC winters fashionably. Quality that lasts.

"I'm not gonna wear a sweater and then a jacket and then a coat."
02 · My Role and Collaboration

I conducted primary and secondary research through a direct interview with her. I designed and iterated based on what she told me, with her as the design subject and feedback source throughout. The project also involved comparing my design against an AI-generated alternative, then co-creating a refined final version with her based on that comparison.

03 · Iterations: From Research to Original Design

Sketch 1: A basic puffer silhouette establishing the high collar, large puffer sections, and thumb loops.

Sketch 2: Added the detachable hood with a structural collar to solve the backpack-catching problem, plus a visible button closure.

Original annotated design: A lighter red puffer with all features labeled: detachable reversible hood, high collar with top button, asymmetrically placed zipper with darker red hardware, large zippered center pockets with darker red hardware, darker red thumb loops with lighter red contrast stitching, and larger puffer sections.

04 · Comparing Against AI

I also prompted an AI (Gemini) to design a coat for her using the same research inputs. The AI produced a navy coat it called the "Kinetic Polymath Parka," with an asymmetrical zip, modular hood, musician's pockets, and ergonomic center pockets.

She preferred my design. She felt that while the AI captured her functional needs accurately, it also included unnecessary details like a dedicated "musician's pocket" just because she mentioned playing violin in the interview transcript, which made her feel strange. The AI also missed that even though she said she liked blue, she already owns blue and black jackets, so I suggested red instead. The AI did not account for her existing wardrobe.

She felt my design addressed her emotional needs: helping her feel put together, confident, and ready for the day through the zipper's sensory cues, supporting her monochrome preference, giving a sense of security with the hood's clicking sound, and feeling fashionable, cute, and cozy.

05 · Co-Created Final Design

After the AI comparison, we co-created a refined version of the coat with four changes:

Longer length to lower hip, for more warmth and coverage.

Buttons added on top of the coat neck area, which she liked from the AI image.

Switched from lighter red to darker red with lighter red details. She likes to feel clean and look clean, and on lighter colors stains show faster. The darker matte red conceals dirt while lighter red hardware and stitching add detail.

Hand zipper pockets moved slightly to the side as they were too centered in the original design.

06 · Final Design Features

Detachable reversible hood: Prevents backpack catching and offers reversible styling. Provides choice and a cohesive monochrome look. Buttons click audibly so she knows when it is secured.

High collar with top button above the collarbone: Eliminates the need for a scarf, keeps her warm, looks fashionable while staying minimal.

Asymmetrical zipper: Allows easy closure, replaces buttons, adds a quirky detail she loves while keeping the monochrome look.

Large zippered pockets positioned to the side (co-created): Keep hands and items secure without straining elbows. Lighter red hardware and monochrome design match her aesthetic.

Darker matte red body (co-created): Conceals dirt and stains, thick insulation and low hip length keep her warm in extreme cold. Favorite color she does not have yet.

Larger puffer sections: Better insulation, feels cute and cozy, boosts confidence.

Quality lighter red zipper: Creates a clear swoosh sound. Signals "ready" when zipping up, "done" when unzipping. Sensory cue adds daily reassurance.

07 · Challenges and Designs Not Pursued

A two-color coat was considered but She said blue and red feels like an American flag. Monochrome was non-negotiable. Button-front closure was explored but she preferred zippers. Thin puff lines were visually tempting but she explicitly wanted larger sections. The AI's navy colorway was not adopted because it did not account for what she already owned. Every eliminated option came from listening more carefully.

08 · What I Learned

Primary research surfaces needs that AI cannot derive from transcripts alone. The AI identified functional requirements accurately but missed the emotional layer: what she already owns, how she wants to feel, and what would make her feel strange rather than seen. The sensory dimension of the zipper sound as a psychological transition ritual, and the importance of the hood click as a cue for assurance, only emerged from genuinely listening rather than pattern-matching interview text.
